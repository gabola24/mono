Project Muse V2: Commercial & Technical Revision

The Niche: The "Creative Ops" Manager for Hybrid Creators

The Pivot: Targeting "Polymaths" is too broad for a go-to-market strategy. To drive sales, we must target an acute pain point. Our Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) is the Multidisciplinary Solo-Creator or Art Director. These users manage video, graphic design, writing, and strategy simultaneously. Their biggest bottleneck is not a lack of ideas, but Context Switching and Asset Fragmentation.

Project Muse is an Agentic Creative Ops Manager. It tracks your emotional bandwidth, organizes your disparate multimodal references, and automates the transition between your distinct creative roles.

✦

Emotion-Aware Routing

Using models evaluated on benchmarks like EQ-Bench [7] or interfaces like Hume AI [8], the app routes you to tasks that match your current cognitive load (e.g., "Low energy? Let's just organize references today").

⚓

Anti-Homogenization RAG

To avoid the severe risk of AI art homogenization [9], the system strictly grounds its ideation in your personal multimodal vector embeddings (your specific sketches, mood boards, and notes), preserving your unique essence.

⚡

Agentic RAG Workflows

Moves beyond simple search. Uses Agentic RAG to perform autonomous planning and multi-hop retrieval [10], synthesizing a project brief from a video snippet and a PDF you saved months ago.

Hard Critique: Viability & Reality Check

A demanding, unbiased look at why apps in this space fail, and how to position this one to succeed.

Strengths (The Hook)

  • Solves the "Blank Page" Paralysis: Translates scattered inspiration into actionable starting points.
  • Personalization Moat: By relying heavily on Multimodal RAG over the user's *own* files, the AI's value increases the more it is used, creating high switching costs.

Fatal Flaws to Avoid

  • The "Data Entry" Problem: Creatives despise logging data. If they must manually type their mood or tag their files, the app is dead on arrival. Fix: Use passive emotional analysis on their voice notes [8] or conversational interactions.
  • The Threat of Homogenization: Relying on AI to "generate concepts" often strips the artist of their agency, leading to generic, culturally misrepresented outputs.[9] Fix: The AI must act as a reflector, not a creator. It should synthesize references, not generate the final art.

The Commercial Verdict

To make people pay for this, it cannot just be a "fun brainstorming toy." It must be positioned as a productivity multiplier for chaotic creatives. You sell them on "getting more done without losing your mind," not just "finding inspiration." Start by targeting freelance designers, video editors, and agency art directors who manage multiple client brand identities.

Execution Phases

  1. Phase 1: The RAG Foundation (src/retrieval). Build the local ChromaDB. Write scripts to embed text and images from a sample folder of your own references. Ensure semantic search works natively via a simple python script.
  2. Phase 2: The Agentic Core (src/agents). Implement the `generator` and `reflector` nodes using LangGraph. The generator suggests a concept based on your query, and the reflector critiques it against your stored references.[11]
  3. Phase 3: The UI (src/ui). Hook the LangGraph logic into a Streamlit chat interface. Implement `st.session_state` to maintain the conversation history and display retrieved images alongside the chat.
